BrandProof: Round 1 Plan
At a glance
BrandProof is a brand-governance platform that checks content against approved sources before it is published, proves every finding, and leaves each final decision to a human.
Product: BrandProof · Tagline: Every finding comes with proof. · Event: Inkloom AI Hackathon, brand governance track · Round 1: plan and idea submission
Five ideas set it apart from a generic AI checker:
1.	Rulebook Compiler. It turns brand PDFs into structured, testable rules, each linked to the page and line it came from. A human approves every rule before it is used.
2.	Hybrid review engine. Fixed checks (terms, colors, contrast, alt text) run first. AI handles only judgement calls such as tone and claim support, so results are faster, cheaper and explainable.
3.	Claim Passport. Every promotional claim must match an approved fact that has an owner and an expiry date. Otherwise, it is flagged as unsupported or outdated.
4.	Intent Lock. The creator state’s purpose, audience, and key message once. Suggestions must keep that intent, and a meaning check shows what would change before anything is applied.
5.	Rule Health insights. The platform finds guidelines that are unclear by tracking which rules are disputed or overridden most, then proposes clearer wording.
1. Understanding of the problem statement
Brand inconsistency is a scale-and-trust problem: more people and AI tools publish faster than any manager can check, so mistakes reach the public before anyone notices.
Organizations now publish through employees, agencies, freelancers and AI writing tools. Each works from a different memory of the brand: an old deck, a chat message, a model's guess. The damage is quiet, because a wrong claim or an off-tone post costs credibility long before anyone corrects it.
Problem in the statement	Why it happens	BrandProof response
Incorrect or outdated claims	Facts change, but old numbers stay in circulation	Approved Facts Vault with an owner and expiry date; every claim must match a fact
Inconsistent language and tone	Guidelines are long PDFs, and vague rules like "friendly but professional" cannot be checked	Rulebook Compiler turns them into structured rules with good and bad examples
Off-brand or inaccessible visuals	Assets are scattered; accessibility is checked late or never	Asset library plus automatic colour, contrast and alt-text checks
Slow, uneven review	Managers read everything by hand	A pre-check clears routine issues so humans review only judgement calls
Creators ignore plain rejections	A "no" with no reason or fix feels arbitrary	Each finding names the rule, shows proof and offers an optional fix
Confidential brand data	Pasting guidelines into outside AI tools can leak them	Workspace isolation, masking and a visible AI-use disclosure
From the statement we draw four rules for any good solution:
•	It checks only against approved sources, never against the model's own opinion of the brand.
•	It explains every finding and names the guideline or fact involved.
•	It suggests, but never overrides, a human decision.
•	It learns where the guidelines themselves are unclear, so the rules improve over time.
2. Users affected by the problem
Six groups feel the problem, and the product is built around the first four, who touch every piece of content.
User	Role in the content flow	Pain today	What they need from BrandProof
Content creators (marketing, sales, support, HR staff)	Write posts, emails, slides, web copy	Unsure of the rules; learn of mistakes only after rejection or publication	Fast, specific, kind feedback before they submit, with a fix they can accept in one click
Agencies and freelancers	Produce content outside the company	Work from outdated guidelines; no access to internal facts	Limited-access workspace with the current rules and facts, and no view of confidential material
Brand and marketing managers	Own consistency and approve content	Read every draft by hand; the same mistakes repeat	Pre-checked drafts, a clear approval queue, and insight into what keeps going wrong
Authorised reviewers (legal, compliance, product experts)	Approve claims in their domain	Asked to check the same claims repeatedly	Review only flagged items, with the source fact attached
Guideline and fact owners	Maintain rules, approved facts, assets	No idea which rules people misread or ignore	Rule Health insights and an easy way to update facts
Leadership and the audience	Bear the reputational risk; read the content	A single false claim damages trust	An audit trail showing that content was checked and who approved it
The most under-served user is the creator. A tool that only rejects content leaves creators guessing; BrandProof is designed to teach as it checks.
3. Proposed solution
BrandProof turns an organisation's approved material into a "Source of Truth", checks every draft against it before publication, and returns findings that cite their proof and offer fixes the creator can accept or reject.
What the system knows
•	Rulebook. Structured rules built from the guidelines. Each has an ID, a category (tone, terminology, messaging, visual, accessibility, claims), a severity, the source page and quote, and one good and one bad example.
•	Approved Facts Vault. Product facts, figures and certifications, each with an owner, a source document and a "valid until" date.
•	Terminology list. Preferred terms, banned terms, spellings and capitalisation.
•	Asset library. Logos, colour palette, fonts and approved images with usage notes.
How the five ideas work
•	Rulebook Compiler. The brand manager uploads guideline PDFs. The system proposes rules, the manager edits and approves them, and only approved rules are ever used in a review. Vague rules are marked "needs examples" rather than guessed at.
•	Hybrid review engine. Code does what code can do exactly: term matching, colour distance, WCAG contrast, missing alt text, readability. AI is called only for tone and claim support, and its output must follow a fixed JSON format that names the rule or fact it relied on.
•	Claim Passport. The system extracts each factual or promotional claim, searches the Facts Vault, and labels it supported, outdated (fact expired), conflicting or unsupported.
•	Intent Lock. Each suggestion is generated with the creator's purpose, audience and key message as constraints. A meaning check then lists the key points before and after the change, so the creator sees nothing important was lost.
•	Rule Health. Every dismissal, override and repeated trigger is logged. Rules that are often disputed are surfaced as unclear, with a proposed rewrite for the manager to approve.

A sample finding (sample brand data, for illustration)
Flagged text	Issue	Proof	Suggested fix
"cuts reporting time by 60%"	Outdated claim	Fact F-07 says 45% and expired on 30 Jun 2026	"cuts reporting time by up to 45%", pending owner re-approval
"the fastest in India"	Unsupported claim	No approved fact supports a ranking	"generates reports in under 5 seconds" (Fact F-12)
"guaranteed"	Banned term	Rule R-21: avoid guarantees in promotional copy	"designed to"
Principles we will not trade away
•	Human in control. AI never edits, approves or publishes on its own. Every suggestion can be accepted, edited or dismissed, and an override needs a short reason that is logged.
•	Explainable. No finding appears without a cited rule or fact. When confidence is low, the finding is labelled "needs human judgement" instead of being stated as fact.
•	Private by design. Each organisation's data is isolated, confidential items are masked before any external AI call, and every review shows which AI service was used and what it saw.
4. Complete product workflow
Content moves through five stages, and a human decision gates every step that matters.
 
workflow · 5 stages, 2 loops, 1 feedback path
Creators fix issues before review, reviewers can send content back, and only the manager's final approval makes content ready for publication.
A. One-time setup (brand manager)
1.	Create the organisation workspace, invite people and assign roles.
2.	Upload guideline PDFs, fact sheets, terminology lists and assets such as logos and the colour palette.
3.	Review the rules proposed by the Rulebook Compiler. Edit, approve or reject each one, and add good and bad examples where a rule is marked "needs examples".
4.	Add each approved fact with an owner, a source document and a "valid until" date.
B. For every piece of content
1.	Draft. The creator pastes text or uploads a document, an image, or a social post (caption plus image). They fill in the Intent Lock: purpose, audience and key message.
2.	Prepare. The system extracts the text (including text inside images), masks anything tagged confidential, and shows what will be sent to any outside AI service.
3.	Pre-check. Fixed checks run first, then AI checks for tone and claims. Findings are sorted by severity, each with its proof.
4.	Fix. The creator accepts, edits or dismisses each suggestion. Dismissing a high-severity finding needs a short reason. The meaning check confirms the Intent Lock still holds, and the content is re-checked.
5.	Review. The creator submits. Reviewers see only the flagged items and what changed since the last version. They comment, approve or request changes, and claims go to the fact owner.
6.	Approve. The brand manager gives final approval. The content becomes "Approved for publication", and its full history (versions, comments, decisions, AI use) is saved and can be exported as a PDF approval record.
C. Learning loop
Findings, dismissals and overrides feed the Rule Health dashboard. Managers use it to clarify rules, renew expiring facts and spot recurring mistakes by team. In the first version, publishing itself happens outside BrandProof.
Roles and permissions
Role	Can do	Cannot do
Creator (staff or agency)	Submit content, resolve findings, comment	Approve content; change rules or facts
Reviewer (for example legal or product)	Review assigned content, approve or request changes, comment	Change rules or facts
Brand manager	Give final approval, edit and approve rules and facts, view insights	Manage users or privacy settings
Admin	Manage users, roles, privacy and AI-use settings	Approve content unless also assigned as a reviewer
No one can approve their own content.

6. Technology stack
We chose Python and React stack on a managed database, so three people can build a full product with tools they can learn quickly and that have large communities.
 
architecture · 3 layers, 1 privacy gate
The web app talks only to our API. The API reads and writes the database and files, and reaches the AI service only through the privacy gate. Arrows run both ways: requests go out and results come back.
Layer	Choice	Why we chose it
Frontend	React, Vite, Tailwind CSS	Fast to build; one app serves all four roles
Backend API	Python with FastAPI	Python has the best text, image and AI libraries; Pydantic validates every AI response
Database, login, files	Supabase: PostgreSQL, Auth, Storage	Roles and workspace isolation through row-level security, without writing login code
Search over rules and facts	Sentence-transformers embeddings (all-MiniLM-L6-v2, runs on a CPU) with pgvector	Finds the nearest rules and facts for each claim, with no extra service
AI reasoning	A hosted large-language-model API, used through structured JSON output	Used only for tone and claim support; wrapped in one interface so the provider can be swapped or replaced by a local model
Fixed text checks	spaCy PhraseMatcher, regular expressions, textstat	Exact, fast, free and explainable
Documents	PyMuPDF (PDF), python-docx (DOCX), python-pptx (slides, stretch)	Standard, well-documented text extraction
Images	Pillow, scikit-learn (dominant colours), WCAG contrast formula, Tesseract OCR	Palette and contrast checks need no AI; OCR lets text in images join the text checks
Version comparison	diff-match-patch	Side-by-side change view between drafts
Hosting and tools	Vercel (frontend), Render (backend), Docker for local setup, GitHub with pytest tests	Free tiers are enough for a demo
How we keep results reliable and explainable
•	Every AI answer must follow a fixed JSON format that names a rule ID or fact ID. A finding without a valid ID is discarded.
•	Every quoted piece of evidence must appear word for word in the draft or in the source. Otherwise the finding is discarded, which blocks most invented evidence.
•	Low-confidence findings are shown as "needs human judgement", not as facts.
•	We keep a "golden set" of 40 to 50 sample drafts with known issues, and measure how many issues we catch and how many false alarms we raise after each change.
How we protect confidential material and disclose AI use
•	Each organisation's data is isolated by row-level security, and files sit in private storage with expiring links.
•	Before any external AI call, a masking step replaces confidential-tagged text, email addresses and phone numbers with placeholders.
•	A disclosure panel on every review shows the provider used, the exact masked text that was sent, and the time.
•	A "rules only" mode runs all fixed checks with no external AI call, for drafts that must not leave the system.
•	Findings are labelled "Rule check" or "AI-assisted", and secret keys stay on the server, never in the browser.

Why this scope is achievable
•	Only three features depend on AI (tone, claim support, suggestions). Everything else is standard code with exact, testable behaviour.
•	Managed services (Supabase, Vercel, Render) remove the need to build login, storage or servers.
•	Each Must feature is a thin slice, and every AI feature has a rules-only fallback.
•	Setup screens accept manual entry and CSV import, so the demo never depends on the Rulebook Compiler working perfectly.
What we will not build
•	Direct publishing to social platforms or content management systems
•	Video and audio review, other than a transcript pasted as text
•	Training our own AI model
•	Languages other than English, a mobile app, and single sign-on

9. Expected impact
BrandProof moves brand checking from after publication to before it, which reduces avoidable errors, lowers reviewer workload and cuts the risk of a damaging public mistake.
Who	Expected impact
Creators	Learn the rules while they write, rewrite less, and keep control of their message through the Intent Lock
Reviewers	See only flagged items with the source attached, and stop re-checking the same claims
Brand managers	Get evidence of what goes wrong and where the guidelines are unclear, and make consistent decisions
The organisation	Publishes fewer incorrect or outdated claims, keeps an auditable approval record, and can use AI writing tools and outside agencies more safely
How we will measure it in the demo
We will test BrandProof on our golden set of 40 to 50 sample drafts with known issues and report the results as they are, including the misses.
•	Detection: the share of known issues that BrandProof catches. Our target is at least 80%.
•	Precision: the share of flagged findings that are valid. Our target is at least 80%.
•	Explainability: the share of findings that cite a rule or an approved fact. Our target is 100%.
•	Review time: minutes to review the same sample drafts by hand and with BrandProof, timed side by side.
10. Future scope after the event
After the event, BrandProof can grow from a pre-publication checker into a brand-governance layer that sits inside the tools people already use.
Near term: meet creators where they work
•	Plug-ins for Google Docs, Word, Slack or Teams, Figma and common website content management systems
•	A browser extension that checks copy in any editor before it is posted
•	Slides, video transcripts and audio, and Indian languages alongside English
Medium term: make the system smarter and safer to change
•	A rule sandbox: replay a proposed rule change on past content to see its effect before approving it
•	Facts connected to their source, such as a product database or pricing page, so facts update at the source and alert owners
•	Stronger visual checks for logo misuse, layout and typography, and reusable brand templates
•	Tuning of rule priorities and tone examples from past reviewer decisions, always approved by a human first
Long term: enterprise scale
•	Single sign-on, detailed audit exports, and private-cloud deployment with locally hosted models for organisations with strict data rules
•	Multiple brands and regional rule variants in one workspace
•	Monitoring of already published pages and campaigns for outdated claims
•	A guided portal for onboarding external agencies
